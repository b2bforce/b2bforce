# Before and after

Our own examples from B2B service firm topics. The facts in "after" are the facts in
"before". When "before" has no fact, "after" does not invent one — it inserts
`[ASK: …]`.

## EN

**EN-C01 + EN-C05**

> It's not just a proposal template. It's a scope contract. And that changes everything.

> The template forces an assumptions section, so the price is tied to what the client must provide.

**EN-O02 + EN-W03**

> It's worth noting that this approach lets agencies leverage their past wins to unlock better proposals.

> Proposals cite past results from the proof library instead of from memory.

**EN-O01 + EN-W02 + EN-C05**

> In today's fast-paced world, a well-defined ICP plays a crucial role in an agency's growth. It decides who you talk to. That's the real win.

> An ICP tells an agency who to talk to and who to turn down. [ASK: a client you turned down, and why]

**EN-C02 + EN-C03**

> Why does this matter? Because clients decide early. The result? Half of churn happens in the first quarter.

> Clients decide early; half of churn falls in the first quarter. [ASK: source for the figure]

**EN-F01**

> - **Speed:** Onboarding is quick.
> - **Security:** Your data stays with you.
> - **Flexibility:** Adapt the process to your firm.

> Onboarding takes [ASK: how many days?], your data stays with you, and the process adapts to your firm.
> ("Before" gives no number of days, so "after" does not invent one.)

**EN-E01 + EN-E02**

> In conclusion, a good proposal is essential. The future belongs to firms that care about scope.

> Next step: take the last proposal that ended in a scope dispute and add an assumptions section to it.

> ICP mówi agencji, z kim rozmawiać, a komu odmówić. [ASK: przykład klienta, któremu odmówiliście, i dlaczego]
