There is a phone call that happens in most mid-market distribution companies
before any large order is confirmed. Someone calls the warehouse and asks what
is actually on the shelf.

That call is not a habit. It is a reconciliation step. The warehouse system,
the webshop, and the ERP each keep their own stock number, and everyone has
learned which one to distrust for which product. The spreadsheet that ops
maintains is not the problem either — it is the patch over the real one.

The real problem is that no system was ever made the single source of truth,
so every department built its own. The cost shows up as invoice lag, as month
ends that need a weekend, and as ordering decisions that wait for a person
instead of a number.

The fix is not a bigger spreadsheet and it is usually not a new ERP. It is
deciding which system owns the truth, integrating the flows that disagree with
it, and making every mismatch surface as an exception someone reviews — instead
of a phone call someone remembers to make.

If your team checks by phone before big orders, that call is worth tracing.
Where it leads is where the integration work is.
