# Persona — CTO, mid-market logistics operator

## Who they are

Runs an engineering team of 8 to 40, reports to the COO or CEO, and owns both the
roadmap and uptime. Came up through engineering and still reads pull requests.
Between five and fifteen years at the company, so the platform's history is
personal.

## What they are accountable for

Shipping the roadmap the business agreed to, and not being the reason a shipper
leaves. Those two pull against each other every release weekend, which is where this
service enters the conversation.

## What they judge success by

Whether their two senior engineers are working on the roadmap instead of regression,
and whether they can take a week off during a release cycle without arranging cover.

## How they buy

Reactive, and fast once a trigger has fired. They will not run a formal RFP at this
size, but they will ask for a reference call with someone who had the same problem.
They read the scope boundaries before the price.

## Objections in their words

- "I have budget for people, not for consultants."
- "How is this different from the pipeline my team could build in a quiet month?"
- "What happens when you leave?"

## What convinces them

A named engagement at a comparable operator, the handover mechanism stated
concretely, and an honest out-of-scope list. A proposal that promises a defect-rate
percentage reads as a vendor who does not understand where the number comes from.

## Where they are reachable

Industry operations conferences, peer conversations with other logistics CTOs, and
long-form writing that names the weekend release problem specifically. Not
responsive to cold sequences that open with a generic efficiency claim.
